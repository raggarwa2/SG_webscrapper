## Acuvue

The scraped data confirms several aspects of the reference research framework, particularly regarding identified purchase barriers. A prominent finding is the matching top barrier of product experience, highlighting issues such as discomfort, dryness, and specific vision quality challenges. This aligns well with the reference framework, which suggests that product-related experiences heavily influence consumer perceptions. Furthermore, the mention of price and channel costs as another significant barrier corroborates the framework's emphasis on economic factors impacting purchase decisions.

However, there are notable divergences that merit further exploration. For instance, reviews indicate a lack of mention for any barrier match across all scraped text, contrary to the reference framework's assertion that there should be a range of identified barriers. This discrepancy suggests a potential gap in the framework's coverage or a unique characteristic of the Acuvue brand experience that requires further investigation.

Another divergence arises from the MyACUVUE app reviews, where multiple complaints about registration friction and app utility were highlighted. This finding contradicts the reference framework's focus on core barriers directly related to product experience and pricing. The hypothesis could be formed that if users are facing significant friction with app functionalities, it may hinder overall brand loyalty and satisfaction.

Additionally, the recurring praise for fast delivery and good packaging represents an aspect not captured by the reference framework, which focuses on barriers. This suggests there may be potential differentiators that enhance customer experience—worth testing the hypothesis that operational efficiencies in delivery and packaging contribute positively to consumer attitudes toward the brand but have been overlooked in the current framework.

Lastly, the distribution of attributes mentioned in the attribute-importance quadrant reveals a notable presence of "Key Drivers." Specifically, the 18 mentions stand in contrast to only 3 for Basic Requirements, suggesting that consumers prioritize aspects beyond the foundational attributes. It may be worthwhile to test the hypothesis that the Acuvue brand significantly relies on these Key Drivers for consumer retention, which may not be adequately represented in the current reference framework analysis.

## Bausch & Lomb

The data scraped from reviews and social posts for Bausch & Lomb largely confirms the reference research framework, particularly in identifying product experience as a top barrier expressed by consumers. Complaints about discomfort, dryness, and specific vision quality issues like astigmatism and presbyopia resonate strongly with the channel taxonomy insights, denoting a clear priority for addressing these negative experiences to enhance customer satisfaction.

However, there are notable divergences that warrant further exploration. Hypothesis: reviews suggest that Bausch & Lomb's loyalty program dissatisfaction is a widespread issue, challenging the effectiveness observed in the reference framework — indicating a potential need to reevaluate reward structures or communications.

Moreover, the pricing-related concerns within the scraped data highlight a discrepancy in perceived value that may not align with expectations set by the reference framework. While consumers noted higher prices and availability of cheaper alternatives elsewhere, this was not emphasized as a prevailing barrier in the framework. Hypothesis: reviews suggest that brand price perception differs widely from what is represented in the reference framework, pointing to significance in competitive pricing strategies — a gap worth evaluating for strategic adjustments.

Lastly, while the lack of professional guidance surfaced as a mention in reviews, its impact across customer journeys may not have been fully captured in the reference framework. Hypothesis: reviews suggest that consumers’ desire for professional guidance is underrepresented in the reference framework, indicating that better support and communication could lead to enhanced customer confidence and reduced purchasing hesitation — calling for a reevaluation of how guidance needs are addressed in marketing and sales.
